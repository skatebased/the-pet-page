What this is

Every community in America has three things: a local newspaper, a local animal shelter, and people who are irrationally devoted to their pets. Almost nowhere are those three things connected to each other in a way that produces money.

The Middleburg Page runs a full page once a month. One side is a rescue story reported from our local shelter. The other side is a story about pet care and pet nutrition, produced centrally and shared with every participating paper. Along the bottom runs a row of eight reader-submitted pet photographs, and each of those photographs is paid for.

That last part is the part that matters. The page does not depend on a national advertiser renewing. It is funded by the readers whose dogs are on it.

The qualifying test

This works in any market that has a community newspaper, a local animal shelter, and at least one independent pet food or pet supply store. If your town has all three, it works. If it's missing one, it probably doesn't.

What you supply, what we supply

We supply — free

  • The monthly pet care and nutrition story, professionally written and fact-checked
  • The full page template, built and ready to drop your content into
  • The four output renders: print page, phone, web and 9:16 video
  • The postcard artwork, press-ready, for your local pet stores to hand out
  • The sell sheet for the reader photo slots
  • The editorial disclosure standard, so your board has something to point at

You supply — local

  • One rescue story a month from your local shelter, 300 words
  • A photograph of the animal
  • Eight reader pet photos, sold at your own rate
  • The page in your paper
  • A relationship with your shelter — most already want this

The division is deliberate. The expensive, specialised work — the national content, the design system, the technical build — happens once, centrally, and costs you nothing. The work only you can do, because only you have it, is local: your shelter, your readers, your pages.

At $375 a slot, eight slots a page, reader revenue alone is roughly double the national sponsorship. If the page only works when a national brand is paying, it isn't a business, it's a favour, and it ends when the sponsorship does.

Four outputs, one source

A newspaper page is a PDF, and a PDF on a phone is unreadable. So the page is not the source — it's one render of it. The story is written once as structured content, and the print page, the phone view, the postcard and the video are all generated from it.

Print pageLive space 9.5 × 10.5 in, 600–1200 DPI, InDesign. Drops into your existing production workflow unchanged.
PhoneSingle column, mobile-first. This is where most of your readers will actually read it — treat it as the primary render, not the leftover.
WebResponsive, two-column above 900 px. Same file as the phone view. This is also the only render that produces hard traffic numbers.
Postcard6 × 4.25 in with bleed and safe zones, press-ready. For your local pet food store's counter, and for USPS EDDM if you want the reach.
Video1080 × 1920 for Reels and Shorts, with platform safe zones marked and captions burned in. Animate the part nobody filmed — the animal's life before rescue.
Jump pageWhen the story runs long in print, it continues on the web at a stable URL. Print the QR code; count the scans.

The part that protects you

A sponsored page carrying reported journalism only works if the wall between them is explicit. Ours is printed on the page itself, in the same size type as everything else:

Printed disclosure

Mars Petcare funds this page and reviews only its own company content. The paper retains full editorial control of all rescue reporting.

The sponsor approves its own copy. It sees the rescue reporting when your readers do. This is not a courtesy to the sponsor — it is the thing that lets you run the page at all, and it is what your board will ask about first. Disclosed plainly, this is a model any editor can defend. Left implicit, it is a liability that travels with every copy you print.

How to start

  1. Call your shelter. Ask if they'll commit to one story and one photograph a month. In our experience they say yes before you finish the sentence — coverage is the thing they want most and can least afford to buy.
  2. Price your slots. $375 is the Middleburg number and it reflects Middleburg. Set yours to what your market bears; the mechanic works at $100 and it works at $500.
  3. Tell us your launch month. We send the template, the national story and the postcard artwork on a monthly cadence.
  4. Sell the first eight slots before you print. The page pays for itself in advance or it doesn't run. That discipline is what keeps this from becoming another unfunded good idea.
  5. Put the QR code on the page and watch the number. Web traffic is the only proof of readership that a national sponsor will accept without argument. Start counting from issue one.
